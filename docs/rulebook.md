# The rulebook

Version 0. Everything here is a working hypothesis, drawn from the desk, looking up, and meant to be challenged. The lower floors are drawn from the inside, the higher ones from models and from people who've been there. When the game tests a rule and it does not hold, or when someone who works there corrects it, the rule changes here, and the change says why.

**Your field is what you know. Your floor is what you decide.**

## The floors

Each floor takes back one question about the work. Below you, the floor underneath keeps answering its question with a rule you can see. In a real organisation these are called decision rights.

| Floor | Question | Discipline | In hand | Time horizon | What you stop seeing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 · The Desk | How? | Execution | a ticket | the hour | nothing yet |
| 1 · The Backlog | What, and in what order? | Backlog management | a board | the day | nothing yet |
| 2 · The Planner | Who, and when? | Capacity planning | a calendar | the day | a person becomes a line in a schedule |
| 3 · The Roadmap | What for? | Project management | a roadmap | the week | tasks, swallowed by projects |
| 4 · The Portfolio | Why this one? | Portfolio management | a set of bets | the quarter | people become "capacity" |
| 5 · The Playbook | By which rule? | Process design | a playbook | the quarter | cards: rules move them now |
| 6 · The Org Chart | Who decides? | Organization design | an org chart | the quarter | people, behind their managers |
| 7 · The P&L | Does it pay? | Business operations, finance | a spreadsheet | the year | "0.8 FTE", then a pixel |
| 8 · The Board | Who owns the company? | Governance and capital | the company | the year | |

### What opens the next floor

Each floor ends with a crisis that the next one answers:

- 0: someone else does your work faster;
- 1: one person is no longer enough;
- 2: the work grows bigger than a card;
- 3: projects compete for the same people;
- 4: you become the bottleneck of every decision;
- 5: you can no longer watch everything;
- 6: the boards stop talking to each other;
- 7: the machine works, and someone wants to buy it.

These crises are borrowed from the real world, in particular Greiner's growth model, Elliott Jaques's levels of work and Brooks's law.

### The two edges of abstraction

Seeing every field as the same flow is the view from the top floor. It is true and useful for running a company, and it also makes people interchangeable. The game teaches both the power of that view and its cost. Placing yourself on the map helps you choose where to go. Placing other people on it is already the view from the top.

## The fields

Every field has the same structure: work arrives, waits in a queue, gets done by people with limited attention, and comes back as feedback. Fields differ on four axes: how checkable the work is, how fast the feedback comes, how much it varies, and what form its value takes.

| Field | Its backlog | What good work means | How fast you know | What an agent is worth | What you learn |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tech | Tickets | It works, and it lasts | Hours (tests) | A lot: code is checkable | Scoping, review, technical debt |
| Product | User stories, specs | The right thing gets built | Days | Little: it guesses without a clear intent | Choosing, cutting, saying no |
| Marketing | A content calendar | People come, and stay | Weeks | Volume, but it turns generic fast | Audience, message, funnel |
| Support | A queue of requests | The customer's problem is gone | Minutes | A lot: solved or not | Listening, triage, the real bugs |
| Design | Design requests | It feels right to use | Days | Little: taste is hard to check | Judgment, consistency |
| Sales | A pipeline of leads | A deal signed, and paid | Weeks to months | Little: trust is human | Trust, timing, closing |

The more checkable the work, the more an agent is worth there. That is why Tech feels the change first.

## Rules we think hold

1. **Every number has its recipe.** You always know why something goes up or down.
2. **The problem before the method.** A method opens only once you have felt the problem it solves.
3. **Humans and agents complement each other.** Agents bring volume and the night; humans bring care, scoping and judgment.
4. **A better system beats a better model.** Humans and agents plug into the same board; quality comes from how you run it.
5. **Real words only.** When the real term exists (Kanban, WIP, capacity, decision rights, P&L), we use it.
6. **You learn a field, not a job.** You learn it by doing the job first; then the job gets delegated, and the field stays yours.
7. **The drift comes from the rules.** Nothing is scripted to make you treat people as numbers; it is simply the efficient path, and you can resist it at a visible price.

## What we don't know yet

- Floors 4 (The Portfolio) and 8 (The Board) are the least grounded in real gestures.
- Design and Sales each need a rule of their own before they can be played.
- The four axes of a field are rough guesses: people who work in that field should correct them.
