# Contributing

Thank you for looking. The game's code is private for now, so the most useful contributions are about the map: how work really runs, floor by floor and field by field.

## Four ways to help

1. **Star the repository.** It tells us whether this is worth building.
2. **Propose a field.** Open an issue with the "Propose a field" form and describe your work: what its backlog holds, what good work means there, how fast you find out whether it was good, what an AI agent is worth in it, and what you learn by doing it.
3. **Challenge a floor.** If a floor's question, its tools, its time horizon or what you stop seeing up there sounds wrong, open a "Challenge a floor" issue and say why.
4. **Share a rule from your work.** The kind nobody writes down: "a customer who waits more than a day never comes back", "nobody reviews on Fridays". Use the "Share a rule" form.

Pull requests on `docs/` are welcome too. Keep one idea per issue or pull request.

## What makes a good contribution

- **Real words.** If your field has a term for it, use it, and say what it means.
- **Your own place.** Describe your work and where you stand. Never rank other people or other jobs.
- **Something you have lived.** One true example is worth more than a theory.
- **Nothing confidential.** No names of colleagues or clients, no internal figures, nothing your employer would not want public.

## How contributions are used

What holds up in discussion goes into [docs/floor-plan.md](docs/floor-plan.md), and from there into the game, where every rule gets tested in play. When a rule does not survive play, it changes, and the issue says why.

By contributing, you agree that your contribution is published under the repository's license, [CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE).

## Conduct

Be kind, be specific, and assume good faith. Criticise ideas, not people. Harassment, discrimination or personal attacks lead to removal from the project.
