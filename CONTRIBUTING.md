# Contributing

Thank you for looking. The game's code is private for now, so the most useful contributions are about the map: how work really runs, floor by floor and field by field. You don't need to code. You need to know your work.

## Five ways to help

1. **Star the repository.** It tells us whether this is worth building. Watch its releases to hear first when something is playable.
2. **Propose a cell.** One field at one floor: what gets decided there, with an example you have lived. Use the "Propose a cell" form.
3. **Share a rule from your work.** The kind nobody writes down: "a customer who waits more than a day never comes back", "nobody reviews on Fridays". Use the "Share a rule" form.
4. **Propose a field.** Open an issue with the "Propose a field" form and describe your work: what its backlog holds, what good work means there, how fast you find out whether it was good, what an AI agent is worth in it, and what you learn by doing it.
5. **Challenge a floor.** If a floor's question, its tools, its time horizon or what you stop seeing up there sounds wrong, open a "Challenge a floor" issue and say why.

Pull requests on `docs/` are welcome too. Keep one idea per issue or pull request.

## What makes a good contribution

- **Real words.** If your field has a term for it, use it, and say what it means.
- **Your own place.** Describe your work and where you stand. Never rank other people or other jobs.
- **Something you have lived.** One true example is worth more than a theory. A good example says where it happened (without names), what was decided, what happened next, and how it differs from the map.
- **Nothing confidential.** No names of colleagues or clients, no internal figures, nothing your employer would not want public.

## Contribute with your agent

If you use an AI assistant, let it interview you and draft the issue. [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) tells it the rules of this repository. Paste this to start:

```text
I want to contribute to The Backlog, an open map of how work runs:
https://github.com/thevzion/the-backlog

Read its AGENTS.md and docs/rulebook.md first. Then interview me, one
question at a time, about my own work: my field, the floor I work on,
one decision I have seen made there, and what happened next.

When you have enough, draft one issue with the matching form:
Propose a cell, Share a rule, Propose a field or Challenge a floor.
Use only what I told you. Remove names, clients and internal figures.
Show me the draft before anything is posted.
```

The example has to be yours. The agent helps you write it down; it doesn't invent it.

## How contributions are used

What holds up in discussion goes into [the rulebook](docs/rulebook.md), and from there into the game, where every rule gets tested in play. Each change links the issue it came from, so who brought it stays visible. When a rule does not survive play, it changes, and the change says why.

Every rule in the rulebook carries a status: `hypothesis`, `discussed`, `in play` or `seen at work`. A rule moves up when it holds in discussion, gets built into the game, or is confirmed by people who live it.

By contributing, you agree that your contribution is published under the repository's license, [CC BY-SA 4.0](LICENSE).

## Who decides

For now, the maintainer decides what goes into the map, and says why in the issue or the commit. A proposal goes in when it brings a lived example, uses the real words of its field, and either fits the rest of the map or gives a reason to change it. Disagreement is welcome: say what you have seen instead.

As more people draw the map, this will change, and the change will say why too.

## Conduct

Be kind, be specific, and assume good faith. Criticise ideas, not people. Harassment, discrimination or personal attacks lead to removal from the project.
